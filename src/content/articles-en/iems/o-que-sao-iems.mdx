<Lead>
An IEM, or in-ear monitor, is an earphone that enters the ear canal and seals it. That simple description hides a profound change in the physics of listening: with the canal closed, the earphone stops being a small speaker near the ear and forms, together with your ear, a single acoustic system.
</Lead>

<Callout type="simples">
IEMs seal your ear canal like an earplug with a speaker inside. That blocks outside noise, makes deep bass easy and means the fit matters enormously. New to this? [Your first IEM](/inicio/seu-primeiro-iem) is a practical guide.
</Callout>

## Where they came from

The name comes from the stage. For decades, musicians heard themselves through floor **monitors** (*wedges*) pointed at them, powerful speakers competing with the drums, the amplifiers and the audience. The result was extremely high volume, feedback and early hearing loss for many musicians. In the 1980s and 1990s, sound engineers began building **in-ear monitors**: earphones molded to the musician's ear, which isolated stage noise and delivered an individual mix at lower volumes.

Around the same time, Etymotic Research, born of hearing aid research, launched consumer IEMs with balanced armatures and a declared focus on measured fidelity, in the early 1990s. The rest is the explosion we know: from earphones bundled with phones to IEMs with dozens of drivers and, more recently, affordable models with excellent measurements.

## What changes when the canal is sealed

<Diagram id="iem-anatomy" />

**1. The outer ear leaves the equation.** The pinna and concha no longer take part. All the acoustic gain they produced, the [pinna gain](/percepcao/pinna-gain), has to be recreated by the IEM itself.

**2. The canal's resonance changes.** Open at one end, the canal resonates as a quarter-wave tube near 3 kHz. Closed at both ends (by the IEM and the eardrum), that resonance disappears and another arises, a half-wave one, at a higher frequency that depends on insertion depth.

**3. Bass becomes a matter of pressure.** In a closed volume of 1–2 cm³, low-frequency pressure is proportional to the diaphragm's displacement, not its acceleration as in a speaker. That's why tiny drivers reproduce 20 Hz, provided the seal is good. See [transducers](/drivers/transdutores).

**4. Passive isolation appears.** The eartip blocks external sound. A well-sealed IEM attenuates the environment by 20 to 35 dB in the mids and treble, more than many ordinary earplugs. In the bass, passive isolation is lower (low-frequency sound passes more easily through tissue), and that's where active noise cancellation helps.

**5. The occlusion effect.** With the canal closed, sounds produced inside the body (your own voice, footsteps, chewing) become louder and boomier, because the energy vibrating through the skull bones no longer escapes through the open canal. Deeper insertions reduce this effect, since the eartip then seals the bony part of the canal, which vibrates less.

## IEMs, earbuds and headphones

| | IEM | Earbud | Headphone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coupling | Seals the canal | Rests in the concha, no seal | Surrounds or rests on the ear |
| Bass | By pressure; depends on seal | Limited by leakage | Depends on pads and chamber |
| Isolation | High (20–35 dB) | Low | Low (open) to medium (closed) |
| Interaction with pinna | None | Partial | Large, varies with placement |
| Variation between people | Mostly treble (insertion) | Bass (fit) and treble | Treble (ear shape, position) |
| Portability | Maximum | High | Low to medium |

## What makes a good IEM

From what we've seen in previous modules, we can list the factors in approximate order of impact:

1. **Fit and seal**: without them, nothing else matters, since bass depends on the seal and treble response depends on insertion.
2. **Frequency response**: close to a target that works for you, and smooth (no aggressive narrow peaks).
3. **Comfort**: an IEM you can't wear for more than 30 minutes is no use.
4. **Low distortion** at normal levels, especially in the bass.
5. **Channel matching** (left and right similar).
6. **Build and durability**: detachable cable, sturdy connectors, nozzle filters.
7. **Compatibility with the source**: adequate sensitivity; impedance that doesn't interact badly with high-output-impedance sources.

Driver count, exotic materials and "premium" cables don't appear on this list, and the rest of this module explains why.
