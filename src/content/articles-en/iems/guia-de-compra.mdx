There's no "best IEM." There's the IEM that seals well in your ear, is comfortable for hours, has a tonal balance close to what you like (or that you can adjust), works with your source and stands up to your use. This guide organizes those priorities, with no rankings and no brands.

## The priorities, in order

### 1. Fit and comfort

This is the criterion that weighs the most and appears least in reviews. An IEM that doesn't seal well loses bass and becomes inconsistent; one that hurts won't be used. Consider:

- **Shell** and **nozzle** size (diameter and length) relative to your ear;
- **weight** and shape ("ergonomic" shells that fill the concha vs. cylindrical models);
- the shop's **return policy**, if you have small ears or narrow canals;
- **included eartips** and the ease of finding others compatible with the nozzle.

### 2. Tonality

Use independent measurements and your personal target (see [your curve](/resposta/sua-curva)). Look for:

- small **broad** deviations (up to ~2 dB) from your target between 100 Hz and 5 kHz;
- no strong **narrow peaks** between 5 and 10 kHz (candidates for sibilance);
- a bass shelf that ends before the lower mids, if you like bass without losing clarity.

If you're willing to use EQ, the criterion becomes more flexible: prioritize low distortion and a good fit, and correct the broad balance afterward.

### 3. Isolation

Essential for transport and noisy environments: more isolation means listening more quietly with the same detail. Lightly vented shells and foam eartips isolate more. Heavily vented IEMs (common with dynamic drivers) isolate a little less.

### 4. Compatibility with the source

Check the sensitivity in dB/V and use the [power calculator](/eletronica/potencia-e-sensibilidade). With phones and ordinary dongles, nearly all IEMs work well. Exceptions: low-sensitivity IEMs (they need more voltage) and multi-BA IEMs with highly varying impedance connected to high-output-impedance sources.

### 5. Build and durability

Detachable cable, sturdy connectors (0.78 mm 2-pin usually withstands frequent changes well; MMCX is practical but can wear out), nozzle filters that protect against wax, warranty and availability of parts.

### 6. Price

Measured performance improves quickly in the entry ranges and, above a certain point, almost stops improving. Higher ranges bring finish, materials, accessories, tuning variations and brand, which may have value for you but are no guarantee of better sound.

## What's usually not worth considering

- **Driver count** as a quality criterion.
- **Expensive cables** as a sound improvement.
- **Spec sheets** without measurements (frequency ranges without tolerance, THD without level).
- **Absolute rankings** based on opinion; use them, at most, as a list of candidates whose measurements you can check.

## Before buying, a checklist

1. Is there an independent measurement, on the rig of a reviewer who has measured other IEMs you know?
2. Is the curve close to your target, or close enough to correct with a few filters?
3. Is the sensitivity suitable for your source?
4. Do the shell and nozzle seem compatible with your ear? Can you exchange it if it doesn't fit?
5. Is the cable detachable, and is the connector a common standard?
