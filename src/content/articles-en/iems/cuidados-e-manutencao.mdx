## Wax and the nozzle

**Earwax** is continually produced by the ear canal and tends to build up on the nozzle and in the bore of the eartip. Even a thin layer over the nozzle filter acts as an additional damper: the treble drops first, then the volume. "My IEM went muffled on one side" is, in the vast majority of cases, wax.

**How to clean:**

- Remove the eartips and clean the nozzle with a soft, dry brush, with the IEM facing down (so dirt falls out rather than in).
- For stubborn residue, use a proper cleaning tool (a fine loop) or cleaning putty, taking care not to push the dirt inside.
- **Don't use liquids** directly on the nozzle. Isopropyl alcohol can be used sparingly on a cloth, only on the outer surface of the shell.
- Many IEMs have **replaceable nozzle filters** (small screens). Changing the filter usually fixes a muffled side.

## Eartips

Wash silicone eartips periodically with warm water and mild soap, rinse and let them dry completely before refitting. Foam eartips shouldn't be washed often (they lose their properties) and need replacing every few months of regular use, when they harden or stop expanding. Dirty eartips worsen the seal and hygiene.

## Moisture

Sweat and condensation can reach drivers (mainly BAs, with their tiny openings) and connectors. After intense use or in humid weather, let the IEMs air-dry, outside a closed case. Cases with silica sachets help in very humid places. Avoid putting wet IEMs in your pocket.

## Cables and connectors

- Avoid winding the cable tightly around the IEM or phone; prefer wide loops.
- When disconnecting, pull by the connector, never the wire.
- **MMCX**: practical rotating connectors, but they can loosen with many changes. Unplug along the axis, without forcing sideways.
- **0.78 mm 2-pin**: check the polarity (there's usually a marking) and don't force it when plugging in.
- Crackling or dropouts when moving the cable indicate a poor connector contact or a broken wire near the plug, the point that suffers most.

## Usage habits

- **Don't sleep with IEMs:** prolonged pressure can irritate the canal and damage the IEM.
- **Don't share eartips** without cleaning them (hygiene).
- **Insert and remove gently**, twisting slightly; this also reduces the pressure effect on the eardrum and *driver flex* in dynamics.
- **Store in the case** to avoid drops, crushing and dirt.

## Your hearing

The most valuable component of your system is your cochlea, and it has no spare parts. Prefer moderate volumes, make use of isolation so you don't need high volume in noisy places, and take breaks. The full subject is in [hearing health](/percepcao/saude-auditiva).

<Callout type="atencao">
Pain, persistent itching, discharge or a feeling of a blocked ear that doesn't go away merit a medical evaluation. Don't use IEMs with an ear infection. Excess wax should be removed by a professional; don't use cotton swabs inside the canal.
</Callout>
