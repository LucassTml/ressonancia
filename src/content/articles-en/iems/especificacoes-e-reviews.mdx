<Lead>
The box of an IEM promises "response from 5 Hz to 40 kHz," "16 Ω," "110 dB," "4 drivers." The review says the sound is "holographic, with textured bass and sparkling treble." How do you turn numbers and adjectives into useful information?
</Lead>

<Callout type="simples">
The numbers on the box tell you little by themselves. An independent measurement tells you much more. And reviews are useful once you know the reviewer's taste, which you can learn by comparing what they write with measurements of earphones they've reviewed.
</Callout>

## The spec sheet

**Frequency response ("20 Hz – 20 kHz").** Without a tolerance (for example, "±3 dB") and without a graph, the number says practically nothing. Almost any transducer produces *some* sound from 5 Hz to 40 kHz, 30 dB below the rest. Ignore the range; look for the graph.

**Impedance ("16 Ω").** The nominal value tells you how much current the headphone will draw for a given voltage. It doesn't say whether the headphone is easy to drive (that depends on sensitivity) and, in multi-BA IEMs, it hides an impedance that varies a lot with frequency. Useful for estimating interaction with the source.

**Sensitivity ("110 dB").** Essential, if the unit is declared. **dB/mW** and **dB/V** differ by 10·log₁₀(1000/Z): for 16 Ω, 18 dB. A "110 dB" with no unit can mean very different things. Use the [power calculator](/eletronica/potencia-e-sensibilidade) to convert and find out how much voltage the IEM needs.

**THD ("&lt; 1%").** Normally measured at 1 kHz and at an unspecified level. It indicates little; the relevant distortion is usually in the bass and at high levels.

**Drivers ("4 BA + 1 DD").** Describes the architecture, not the quality. See [multi-driver](/drivers/multi-driver-e-crossovers).

**Materials ("beryllium diaphragm," "titanium shell," "8-wire silver cable").** They can affect durability, weight and, in the case of the diaphragm, the driver's behavior, but the audible effect only shows up in a measurement of the whole.

## Independent measurements

The most informative document about an IEM is a **frequency response measurement from an independent source**, ideally:

- made on an IEC 60318-4 coupler or B&K 5128, with the equipment declared;
- with both channels;
- with smoothing and normalization declared;
- compared with a target in the **same domain**.

Measurement databases (from individual reviewers and collaborative projects) let you compare hundreds of IEMs measured on the same equipment, and that "same reviewer, same equipment" comparison is the only legitimate one, as explained in [how a headphone is measured](/resposta/como-se-mede).

Useful complementary measurements: **distortion** (THD as a function of frequency, at 90–100 dB SPL), **impedance** as a function of frequency, **channel matching** and sometimes **isolation**.

## How to read a review

Subjective reviews are useful when you know how to **calibrate** them. Some questions:

1. **What's the reviewer's reference?** Someone used to very bright headphones will describe a Harman as "dark." Read other reviews by the same author of headphones whose measurement you know.
2. **How was the listening done?** With what source, what eartip, what volume? Was there a direct comparison, with matched volume? Or is it an isolated impression?
3. **Does the reviewer show measurements?** When impressions and measurement agree, great; when they disagree, the measurement usually better explains what happens below 5 kHz, and the reviewer's anatomy what happens above.
4. **Are there conflicts of interest?** Units received for free, affiliate links and relationships with manufacturers don't invalidate a review, but they should be declared.
5. **Does the text use terms with verifiable meaning?** "Bass at +6 dB invading the lower mids" is verifiable; "bass with velvety texture and holographic blackness" isn't.

## A small dictionary of subjective terms

| Term | Most common interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Warm, full-bodied | Bass and lower mids above target, restrained treble |
| Bright, analytical | Presence and treble above target |
| Dark, veiled | Presence and treble below target |
| V-shaped | Elevated bass and treble, recessed mids |
| Sibilant | Peak between ~5 and 9 kHz |
| Shouty | Excess at ~1.5–3 kHz |
| Muddy | Excess at ~250–500 Hz, or bass invading the mids |
| Airy | Extension and energy above 10 kHz |
| Detailed, resolving | Enough presence and treble, little masking, low distortion |
| Wide stage | Spatial impression; often associated with treble and the 3–10 kHz region |
| Musical | Usually a warmer, more forgiving signature |

The full vocabulary, linked to curves, is in [regions and flaws](/resposta/regioes-e-defeitos).
