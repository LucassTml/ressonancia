## The stages

**The file or stream** stores music as a long list of numbers that describe the position of the wave tens of thousands of times a second.

**The DAC** (digital-to-analog converter) turns those numbers into a continuous electrical signal. There's a DAC in every phone, laptop, USB-C adapter and Bluetooth headphone.

**The amplifier** takes that weak signal and delivers enough energy to move the headphone. On most devices it's also what controls the volume.

**The driver** is the small part inside the headphone that moves the air. It turns electricity back into sound.

**The coupling** is the last stretch, between the headphone and your eardrum. The eartip and the way the earphone sits have a huge effect, and this is the stage that gets the least attention.

**Your ear and brain** complete the line. Everyone has a different ear canal, which is why the same headphone arrives differently for each person.

## Where the sound actually changes

The difference you can hear between the components is very uneven:

| Stage | Weight in the result |
| --- | --- |
| Headphone and tuning | Huge |
| Fit and eartip | Huge |
| Equalization | Large, and usually free |
| Amplifier | Small, if it has enough power and low output impedance |
| DAC | Small; today's cheap ones are very good |
| Cables | Usually none, if they're intact |

<Callout type="chave" title="Where to spend first">
Buy the right earphone, get the eartip right and try some equalization. Only afterward, and if there's a concrete reason like hiss or not enough volume, think about a separate DAC or amplifier. For most IEMs, a cheap USB-C adapter or the device's own output is enough.
</Callout>
