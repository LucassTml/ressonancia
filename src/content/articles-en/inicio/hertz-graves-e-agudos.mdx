## What a hertz is

One **hertz** (Hz) is one repetition per second. If the small part in your headphone goes back and forth 100 times in a second, it makes a 100 Hz sound. When the number gets large we use **kilohertz** (kHz): 1 kHz is a thousand repetitions per second, 3 kHz is three thousand.

Hear three steps on that scale: <Listen label="100 Hz" f="100" /> <Listen label="1 kHz" f="1000" /> <Listen label="8 kHz" f="8000" />.

If the 100 Hz one barely shows up, your hearing isn't at fault: phone and laptop speakers hardly reproduce that region. Use headphones if you can.

## How far we hear

The range is usually quoted as **20 Hz to 20 kHz**. That's an approximation. Almost no adult hears up to 20 kHz, because the upper limit falls naturally with age. Many adults stop around 14 to 16 kHz, and that's normal. In practice it makes little difference to music, since nearly all the important musical information sits well below that.

## The map: bass, mids and treble

Musicians and engineers divide that range into regions. The boundaries are conventions, but the general idea is this:

| Region | Approximate range | What lives there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sub-bass | 20 to 60 Hz | Weight you feel more than you hear |
| Bass | 60 to 250 Hz | Kick drum, bass guitar, the "body" of a song |
| Mids | 250 Hz to 2 kHz | Voices, guitars, piano: most of the notes |
| Presence | 2 to 5 kHz | Vocal clarity; the region where the ear is most sensitive |
| Treble | 5 to 20 kHz | Brightness, cymbals, "air" |

<Callout type="chave" title="Why this matters">
When someone says a headphone "has too much bass" or "is shrill," they're talking about how much sound it makes in one of these regions compared with the others. A headphone's graph is just this map, drawn with numbers.
</Callout>

Notice something strange: the bass spans 60 to 250 Hz, a narrow range in hertz, while the treble spans thousands of hertz. Yet both regions feel equally "big" to our ears. The explanation is the most useful concept in audio: [the octave](/inicio/o-que-e-uma-oitava).
