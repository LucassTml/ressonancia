## Where the measurements come from

Every curve was made by **oratory1990**, with a GRAS RA0045 coupler compliant with the IEC 60318-4 standard, and is published in the **AutoEq** project. Using a single source matters because measurements from different rigs aren't directly comparable.

The dashed line is the **Harman IE 2019** target, the result of listener preference tests. It works as a ruler, not a verdict: straying from the target is often a deliberate choice.

<Callout type="atencao" title="What a curve doesn't say">
Each curve comes from one unit: yours may differ by a few decibels, especially above 6 kHz. And frequency response doesn't describe comfort, build or how the earphone fits your ear. Prices are approximate launch prices and vary a lot.
</Callout>

## Compare freely

Pick up to four models. The music that plays applies to your own headphones the difference between the chosen model and the target, which gives a good idea of each one's character.

## What the price buys

In the chart below, each point is an IEM. The higher it is, the closer it sits to the target between 100 Hz and 5 kHz, the region where measurement is most reliable.

<Lab id="iem-price" />

The result is clear: **there's no relationship between price and closeness to the target**. The closest model costs about US$ 150, the second about US$ 50 and the third about US$ 20. Several of the most expensive are among the farthest away.

That doesn't mean expensive ones are bad. It means that past the entry level, manufacturers use their tuning freedom to make choices of character, and that the price also pays for build, accessories, fit and distortion at high volume.
