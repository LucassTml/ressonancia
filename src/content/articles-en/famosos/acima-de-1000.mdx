## What changes, and what doesn't

The five models below have five different architectures: four balanced armatures in the Shure SE846, five in the Sony IER-M9, a single dynamic in the Sennheiser IE 900, an unusual hybrid in the Sony IER-Z1R and twelve balanced armatures in the 64 Audio A12t. Even so, between 100 Hz and 5 kHz, all of them are further from Harman than a Moondrop Chu.

That doesn't mean these earphones are worse. It means **they weren't made to minimize that measure**. They're after other things: a specific character, stage use, comfort over long sessions, custom molds, build, and the exclusivity of a fine, rare object.

There's also what frequency response doesn't capture well, such as distortion at very high volume and behavior with different output impedances, along with small differences whose audibility hasn't always been demonstrated. The section [Measurable versus audible](/ciencia/mensuravel-vs-audivel) discusses where those limits are.
