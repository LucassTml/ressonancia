<Callout type="simples">
A signature is the overall shape of a headphone's sound, in one word. "Warm" means more bass and less treble, "bright" the reverse, and "V-shaped" means more bass and treble with the mids pushed back. None of them is right or wrong: they're choices, and you can hear each one below.
</Callout>

## What a signature is

A **sound signature** is the general shape of a headphone's frequency response, described relative to a neutral reference. It ignores fine details (narrow peaks, small ripples) and captures the balance between bass, mids and treble, which, as we saw in [reading graphs](/resposta/lendo-graficos), is the most important aspect for perception.

Today, the implicit reference is nearly always a target like [Harman](/resposta/targets-e-harman) or diffuse field. That's important: when someone says a headphone is "neutral," they may mean "close to diffuse field" (no bass shelf) or "close to Harman" (with a shelf). A headphone exactly following Harman will be described as "neutral with bass" by some and as "warm" by those who use diffuse field as a reference.

### Neutral (diffuse field)

No bass lift beyond the diffuse-field balance. Mids and treble in line with the reference. To many listeners it sounds **correct but thin**, especially at low volume and with modern music. It's the traditional signature of studio reference headphones and IEMs like Etymotic's.

### Harman (neutral with bass)

The average preferred target in Harman's tests: modified diffuse field plus a bass shelf of several decibels below ~200 Hz. It sounds balanced and full-bodied to most people; some find it slightly warm, others find it short on bass.

### Warm

Elevated bass and mid-bass, slightly attenuated treble. Full-bodied, soft, "cozy," tolerant of poor recordings and sibilance. In excess, it loses clarity and the mids get thick.

### Bright

Upper mids and treble above target. It highlights attacks, details and texture, which is why it's often associated with "resolution." In excess it's tiring, sounds harsh and emphasizes sibilance and recording flaws.

### Dark

Recessed presence and treble. Relaxing, it allows high volumes without apparent fatigue (which can be a trap for hearing health). In excess it sounds muffled, distant and airless.

### V-shaped

Elevated bass and treble, recessed mids. It's the "exciting" signature of many consumer headphones: impact and brightness up front, voices and melodic instruments further back. It works well with electronic music and modern pop; it usually harms voices, guitars and acoustic music. In quick comparisons in shops, V-shaped headphones often impress, and there lies the danger: impressing in 30 seconds isn't the same as pleasing over three hours.

### U-shaped

A relative of the V, but with the recess spread more gently across the mids and less aggressive. Often described as "fun but balanced."

### Mid-forward

Upper mids elevated around 1–3 kHz. Voices are very present and close. Good for those who prioritize voice and melodic instruments; in excess, shouty.

### Bass-head

A very high, wide bass shelf that invades the lower mids. Maximum impact and pressure. The cost, through [masking](/percepcao/mascaramento), is midrange clarity.

## Signatures aren't value judgments

<Callout type="preferencia">
No signature is "correct" in the abstract. Harman's tests show that most people prefer something close to the target, but also that there are consistent groups who prefer more bass or more treble. The point isn't to find the "right" signature, but **to know which one you prefer and why**, and to recognize that a strong signature can be corrected, to a good extent, by equalization.
</Callout>

There are, however, reasonable arguments for starting from something close to neutral:

- **Versatility.** A neutral headphone with moderate bass works well with more genres. A strong V-shaped one can be great for one genre and bad for another.
- **Fidelity to intent.** Music was mixed on reference systems; a headphone close to them more closely reproduces what the engineers heard.
- **Ease of adjustment.** Starting close to the target and adjusting a few decibels with EQ is easier and cleaner than correcting large deviations.

## A signature isn't everything

Two headphones with the same signature can sound different. The signature describes the broad shape of the curve; details matter too:

- **Smoothness.** Narrow peaks and dips within a "neutral" signature can cause roughness or localized sibilance.
- **The 2 to 10 kHz region** greatly changes the character, even with the same general shape.
- **Distortion, channel matching and seal** are independent of the signature.
- **Your anatomy** can turn a headphone that's "neutral" on the coupler into a bright or dark one in your ear.

Knowing how to name and hear signatures is the first step toward finding out which suits you. The second is to measure that systematically: the subject of [how to find your curve](/resposta/sua-curva).
